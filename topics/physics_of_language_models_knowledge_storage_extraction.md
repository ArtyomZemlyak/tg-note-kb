# Physics of Language Models: Part 3.1, Knowledge Storage and Extraction

## Authors
- Zeyuan Allen-Zhu (Meta / FAIR Labs)
- Yuanzhi Li (Mohamed bin Zayed University of AI)

## Publication
- arXiv:2309.14316
- September 18, 2023 (version 3)
- https://physics.allen-zhu.com/part-3-knowledge/part-3-1

## Overview

This paper investigates how transformer-based language models memorize knowledge during training and extract it during inference. It specifically examines whether models answer knowledge-based questions by extracting information from sources like Wikipedia or by recognizing similar questions from their training data (data contamination).

## Key Research Question

How do language models memorize knowledge during training, and extract it later to answer questions or perform logical reasoning during inference?

The paper clarifies that memorizing all sentences in training data does not ensure that models can extract or manipulate factual knowledge from those sentences during inference. Models can reproduce exact inputs but might not be able to answer factual questions about them.

## Methodology

The researchers used a controlled biography dataset of 100k synthetic biographies to conduct controlled experiments, examining mathematical properties that characterize knowledge representation and extraction.

### Dataset Families
- **bioS**: Synthetic dataset generating profiles for N=100,000 individuals with randomly selected attributes (birth dates, cities, universities, etc.)
- **bioR**: "Close-to-real" dataset generated by Llama with more natural language biographical entries

### QA Framework
For each individual, six questions target unique attributes:
1. Birth date
2. Birth city
3. University
4. Major of study
5. Company worked for
6. Work location

## Main Results

### Result 1: Mixed Training Enables Knowledge Extraction
When models are trained on both biographical data (BIO) and question-answer pairs (QA) simultaneously:
- OOD generalization accuracies were 86.6% for bioS single and 77.7% for bioR single
- Model exhibits abnormal learning behavior, learning from QAs first and then associating with BIO data

### Result 2: BIO Pretrain + QA Finetune Fails
Models pretrained on biographical data alone, then finetuned on QAs:
- Achieve perfect BIO token memorization but zero QA accuracy on out-of-distribution individuals
- Demonstrates that word-by-word memorization doesn't guarantee knowledge extraction capability

### Result 3: Knowledge Augmentation is Critical
Key augmentations that improve knowledge extraction:
- **Multiplicity**: Creating M diverse biography entries for each individual using varied language
- **Permutation**: Randomly shuffling the order of biographical sentences
- **Fullname**: Replacing pronouns with full names throughout biographical entries

With proper augmentation, QA fine-tune accuracy increased from 9.7% to 96.6%.

### Result 4-5: Probing Techniques Explain Knowledge Storage
The paper introduces two probing methods:
- **P-probing (Position-based)**: Probes hidden states at specific positions in biographical text
- **Q-probing (Query-based)**: Probes hidden states of just the person's name

Key finding: Knowledge augmentation pushes models to encode a person's knowledge almost linearly in the model's hidden embedding of the person's name tokens, making extraction possible.

### Result 6: Celebrity Effect Helps Minority
When augmentation is applied only to a "celebrity" subset of individuals, it also improves knowledge extraction for "minority" individuals who weren't augmented. This suggests that diverse representations of some entities can improve general knowledge extraction.

### Result 7: Bidirectional Models Also Face Challenges
BERT-like models (GBERT) struggle with knowledge extraction even with bidirectional attention, unless knowledge consists of standalone, independent words. The MLM pretraining task doesn't necessarily promote proper knowledge storage for extraction.

## Key Findings

### Knowledge Augmentation is Essential
Without knowledge augmentation (paraphrasing, sentence shuffling, translations), models may memorize training data but cannot extract knowledge, leading to 0% accuracy regardless of instruction fine-tuning.

### Linear Encoding of Knowledge
Knowledge augmentation enables models to encode knowledge linearly in the hidden embeddings of entity names, making extraction possible. Without augmentation, knowledge is distributed across all tokens in the text, making extraction nearly impossible.

### Pre-training Data Rewriting
The paper recommends pre-training data rewriting (augmentation) during pre-training rather than addressing this during fine-tuning, which is often too late. Simple tools like Llama-7B or smaller models are adequate for this rewriting task.

### Including Instruction Data Earlier
Including more instruction-finetuning data during pre-training is beneficial. Postponing all QA-like data to the fine-tuning phase is suboptimal.

## Practical Implications

1. **Data Augmentation is Critical**: For knowledge extraction tasks, training data must be properly augmented with diverse expressions of the same knowledge
2. **Quality over Quantity**: Diverse expressions of knowledge matter more than sheer volume of unchanged data
3. **Early Integration**: Instruction tuning data should be integrated earlier in the training process
4. **Linear Representations**: Knowledge should be encoded linearly in entity name embeddings for effective extraction

## Technical Details

The study used GPT2/Llama architectures with rotary positional embeddings, testing various model sizes (124M, 302M, 682M parameters). The researchers employed both full finetuning and LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) approaches.
